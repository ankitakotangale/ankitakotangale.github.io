Research & Scholarship

My Research

My research explores how Design Thinking develops through authentic learning experiences, how it can be meaningfully assessed, and how educators can support its development.

I examine these questions in computing, engineering, and informal STEM learning environments using quantitative, qualitative, and assessment-based research approaches.

Research Project 01

Design Thinking in Computing and Engineering Education

Higher Education Research

This research explores how computing and engineering educators understand Design Thinking and its role in professional practice and education.

Through interviews and qualitative inquiry, the study examines educators' perspectives on relevant Design Thinking skills, design models, and instructional approaches.

Research Project 02

Developing Design Thinking Through Authentic STEM Learning

GERI Design Thinking Study

This study examines how high school learners develop Design Thinking mindsets and demonstrate Design Thinking skills during a two-week informal STEM learning experience.

Students participate in authentic engineering design activities involving user-centered problem-solving, collaboration, prototyping, feedback, and iteration.

The research uses pre- and post-assessments, including a Design Thinking mindset instrument and a scenario-based assessment with a scoring rubric, to examine learning outcomes.

Research Project 03

Design Thinking in FIRST Robotics Learning Environments

FIRST Robotics Research

This study investigates Design Thinking mindset and demonstrated application among students participating in FIRST Robotics programs.

The research considers students' experiences in FTC and FRC robotics environments and uses Design Thinking assessments and scenario-based measures to explore the development and application of Design Thinking.

The study extends Design Thinking assessment into robotics-based informal STEM learning.

Future Research Direction

Human–AI Collaboration in Design Thinking

Proposed Research

My future research interests include exploring how collaboration between humans and AI tools may influence Design Thinking processes, decision-making, and learner agency.

This direction considers how AI-supported learning experiences might be structured to support reflection, iteration, and meaningful human participation in design.
